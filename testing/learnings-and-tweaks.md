## Hypothesis H1: No-click-needed alerts on sign-up and consent pages

**Status: not built.** This is a candidate tweak the team suggested before testing ("you don't have to click on extension", "random pop ups"). This round only checks whether the problem is real and which alert style people would accept; nothing in the extension changes.

- **Problem:** c3nsor only helps if people remember to click the icon before they sign up. At that moment they're focused on finishing the form or closing the cookie banner, not on checking a policy.
- **Hypothesis:** If c3nsor analyzed the site automatically and showed a small alert only at meaningful moments (sign-up, create-account, or cookie-consent pages), more people would see the findings before agreeing, without the annoyance of pop-ups on every site.
- **Options to compare:**
  1. **Click the icon** (how it works today). No automatic analysis.
  2. **Quiet badge only.** The icon shows a count on sign-up and consent pages, with no pop-up. This is the low-friction middle option.
  3. **Alert on sign-up and consent pages only.** A small panel appears at those moments.
  4. **Alert on every site.** This is the "random pop-ups" version, included for contrast; we expect it to be annoying.
- **How we'll validate it this round:**
  - Observation (extension track only): in Task E3 of the session script, testers go to a real sign-up page without being told to use c3nsor. Did they click the icon unprompted? Then: "If I weren't sitting with you, would you have remembered to click c3nsor before finishing this sign-up? Why or why not?"
  - Interview (Try it track): "Next time you sign up for something, would you remember to check it with c3nsor first? How?"
  - Survey question 10 (which option they prefer, shuffled, with an honest note that options 2 to 4 aren't built and need the extension to read pages automatically) and question 11 (how annoying a sign-up-page alert would be, 1 to 5). Every tester answers these.
- **Decision rule (set before testing):** Supported if most testers did not click c3nsor unprompted (or, with no extension-track testers, most say they wouldn't remember to check), at least half prefer option 2 or 3, and median annoyance for option 3 is 2 or lower. Not supported if most testers clicked unprompted or say they'd reliably remember, most prefer option 1, median annoyance is 4 or higher, or testers raise privacy concerns about automatic access.
- **Metrics it should move:** M6 action rate (more analyses happen at the moment of decision) and M7 likelihood to use. A later round would add a "checked before sign-up" rate, still measured through moderated tasks or a diary study, not telemetry.
- **Risks:**
  - **Annoyance:** alerts at the wrong time feel like the pop-ups c3nsor is supposed to protect people from. Misdetected pages (a login page that isn't a sign-up) would make it worse.
  - **Permissions and store review:** automatic analysis means reading pages without a click, which `activeTab` doesn't allow. Requesting all-site host access at install would bring back Chrome's "Read and change all your data on all websites" warning and would likely re-trigger the Chrome Web Store in-depth review that version 0.1.1 was changed to avoid. A possible middle path is an opt-in setting that requests all-site access at runtime through the existing optional host permissions; confirm with Chrome Web Store policy before relying on it.
  - **Privacy perception:** a privacy tool that reads every page can look like the tracking it warns about, even if analysis stays on the device. It must stay telemetry-free and say so plainly.
  - **Detection accuracy:** recognizing sign-up and consent pages reliably is its own rules problem.
- **Effort if pursued:** L.
